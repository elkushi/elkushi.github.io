Get higher on Google Maps and Yandex Maps: what decides the order, and the photos that help

When someone searches "dentist near me", they pick from the map, not from your website. Here is what Google and Yandex say decides the order, the photos a card needs, how to get reviews without breaking the rules, and a 15-minute check you can do today. With examples from about 40 Moscow clinics I studied this autumn.

Why the map card matters more than the site

For a local search, Google and Yandex both show a map with a short list of places, often above the ordinary results. People compare the cards in that list: the rating, the number of reviews, the photos, whether it is open now. Many call or build a route straight from the card and never open the website.

So the card is your first page. A clinic with a great website and a weak card loses the patient before the website gets a chance.

What decides the order

Google says it plainly. Local results are based mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your card matches what the person searched for. A complete, detailed card matches more searches.
  • Distance: how far you are from the person searching. You can't change this one.
  • Prominence: how well known you are. Google counts reviews and ratings ("more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking") and links to your site from other websites.

Google also says: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." Anyone who sells you a guaranteed first place on Google Maps is selling something Google says does not exist.

Yandex does not publish a formula for its maps. What it does publish:

  • The rating is built from all ratings and reviews, but it is not the average. It weighs how many reviews there are and how reliable each reviewer is. A review from someone who has never rated anything before counts for less. It is recalculated every day and appears only after 3 to 5 ratings.
  • Yandex's help for businesses keeps repeating the same advice: keep the information current, publish news and answer reviews. Some card features only open up once a company has enough reviews and good photos and video.
  • Paid placement exists. In the ads on Yandex Maps, the position depends on the bid and on how likely people are to call or build a route. That is an ad slot, and it goes when the payment stops. Organic position comes from the card.
  • Each year Yandex gives the "Good place" (Хорошее место) badge to companies with a rating of 4.5 or higher and information the owner keeps up to date.

What a weak and a strong card look like

Same street, same kind of clinic. Which one would you call?

Step 1: claim and verify the card

Both are free. On Google it is Google Business Profile; Google says verification makes a business more likely to appear in results. On Yandex it is Yandex Business, where a confirmed card shows "Information confirmed by the owner".

Check for duplicates while you are there: an old card at a previous address, or one someone else created. Two cards for one clinic split the reviews between them.

Step 2: fill in every field, the same way everywhere

  1. Name: the one on your sign. Don't add "best dentist Moscow cheap". Google's guidelines forbid adding extra words to the name, and a card can be suspended for it.
  2. Main category: the exact one. "Dentist", not "Medical centre". Then add the extra categories that are true: children's dentistry, implants, orthodontics. Categories decide which searches you can appear for at all.
  3. Hours, including holidays. A card that says "open" on a holiday when the door is locked earns a one-star review.
  4. One phone number, the same as on your site.
  5. The "Website" button points to your own site.
  6. Services and prices, the same as on the site.
  7. Attributes: parking, step-free entrance, card payment. They answer the questions people would otherwise call about.

Items 4 to 6 are where I see the most mistakes. From the clinics I studied this autumn:

  • A clinic with a 5.0 rating and hundreds of reviews on Yandex, whose "Website" button led to an aggregator page with a phone number that is not on the clinic's own site. Some patients who found it on the map called someone else's tracking number.
  • Teeth whitening on the map card at more than one and a half times the price on the site. A patient who sees the higher price first may not look further.

Step 3: photos

Google lists photos and videos among its tips for local ranking, and Yandex ties some card features to "good photos and video". The bigger reason is people. Before they call a clinic, they look at it: is it clean, is it modern, will I find the door?

The shot list

  1. The building from the streetDaylight, the sign readable. Make it the first photo, so people recognise the place when they arrive.
  2. The entranceWhich door, which floor, the intercom. Clinics in residential buildings need this most.
  3. The way inFrom the nearest metro exit or the car park, in two or three shots.
  4. Reception and waiting areaShows the place is calm and tidy.
  5. A treatment roomClean, well lit, the chair and equipment visible.
  6. The doctors and the teamReal people, not stock photos, and only with each person's written consent.
  7. Equipment that sets you apartA scanner, a microscope, a children's room. Only what you actually have.
  8. New photos every few monthsA new doctor, a renovated room, the team at a training. A card that changes looks alive.

Shoot horizontally in daylight with a recent phone; that is enough. Don't put your phone number or prices into the image: the text fields are for that, and they stay correct when you change them.

Step 4: reviews, the honest way

Reviews are the one factor both services name, and the one most clinics leave to chance. A steady flow of real reviews beats a burst of fifty in one week.

  1. Ask every patient, on the same day. At the end of the visit, or in a message that evening, with a direct link to the review form. Google Business Profile has a "Get more reviews" button that gives you a link and a QR code; Google suggests putting it in thank-you messages, at the end of a chat and on receipts.
  2. Ask everyone, not only the happy ones. Google forbids asking only satisfied customers, discouraging negative reviews and setting staff a quota of reviews.
  3. Know the reward rule, because it differs. Google forbids any reward for a review: money, discounts, free services. Yandex's rules allow a discount for a review, but not for a positive one only. If you work with both maps, the simplest safe rule is: no rewards at all.
  4. No reviews from staff, relatives or friends. Yandex counts a review as fake if the author never used the company, or wrote it for money or because friends asked. Google removes reviews written with a conflict of interest, including from employees. Bought reviews also tend to come in bursts from new accounts, and Yandex already gives new reviewers less weight.
  5. Send people from your site to the map. One clinic I studied had a 5.0 rating and hundreds of reviews on Yandex, but its site didn't show that, and its "Leave a review" form saved reviews on the site, where no search engine counts them. A button that opens the map's review form does more.

Reply to every review, the good ones too

Google lists replying among its tips. A reply is also written for the next reader, not only for the author. For a clinic there is one extra rule: never confirm details about a patient. In Russia, even the fact that a person came to a clinic is a medical secret (law 323-FZ, article 13), and most countries have a similar rule.

Review:  "Had to wait 30 minutes past my appointment time."

Weak:    "Ivan Petrov, you came on 12 May for a filling and were late yourself."
Good:    "Thank you for writing this, and we're sorry about the wait.
          We've changed how we book long appointments so the schedule
          doesn't run over. Please ask for the clinic manager on your
          next visit, or call the number on this card."

Step 5: keep the card alive

A card that was filled in once two years ago slowly goes wrong: a doctor leaves, prices change, the holiday hours are never set. Fifteen minutes a week is enough:

  • Answer new reviews and questions.
  • Set hours for the next holiday before it comes.
  • Publish a short update or news item: Yandex Business has posts, Google Business Profile has updates.
  • Add a new photo once a month.
  • After any change on the site (a price, a doctor, the phone), change the cards the same day.

Your website helps the card

The card and the site work as a pair. Google counts links to your site as part of prominence, and the site is where a patient goes to check prices and doctors before booking. Make the two point at each other:

  • The card's "Website" button opens your site; your contacts page links to both map cards and shows the same address and phone.
  • Add structured data with the same details, so search engines can match the site to the card. For a dental clinic, the type is Dentist:

What doesn't work

  • Keywords in the name. Against Google's guidelines, and a reason for suspension.
  • Bought or swapped reviews. They get removed, and the rating counts new accounts for less anyway.
  • A second card for the same address to "take two places". It splits your reviews and both cards can be removed.
  • Stock photos. People notice, and they trust the card less.
  • Paying for "a guaranteed first place" in the organic list. On Google it doesn't exist; on Yandex the paid place is an ad that ends when you stop paying.

A 15-minute check you can do today

  1. Search like a patient. On a phone, search for your service and area in both maps, for example "dentist Belorusskaya". Results depend on where the searcher is, so search from a nearby street, not from your own reception. Where are you, and who is above you?
  2. Look at the cards above yours. Count their photos and reviews, and check whether they reply.
  3. Open your own card. Is it verified? Is the first photo your entrance?
  4. Tap "Call" and "Website". Do they reach you, or someone else?
  5. Compare three prices on the card with the same prices on your site.
  6. Read your last 10 reviews. How many have a reply?

If step 1 shows competitors with twice your photos and replies under every review, steps 3 to 6 usually show why.
